**Verifact**

**Fact check: Did Twitter reverse its X rebrand after 14 days?**

*By Liesel Reinholt, Verifact fact-checker*
*Edited by Hector Bianchi*
*September 2023*

A screenshot showing the blue bird logo back on a Twitter-style homepage has circulated widely on social media in recent weeks. The image is captioned with a claim that the company reversed its rebrand to "X" after only 14 days, restoring the original name and bird. Some posts say the change was "quietly undone" in early August. Others share the screenshot without comment.

The rebrand itself is real. Starting in late July 2023, the platform formerly known as Twitter began moving its web address and visual identity to X, and the familiar bird was removed from its main logo. The claim we are checking concerns what happened next. Our conclusion is that the reversal did not happen. The screenshot is an edited image, and the records we checked show the X branding in place on 7 August and afterward.

This article explains how we reached that conclusion and, more importantly, how readers can check similar images themselves.

**What the claim says**

The claim has two parts. The first is that the rebrand began in late July. The second, and the one that matters here, is that it was reversed about two weeks later, on or around 7 August, with the original Twitter name and bird logo returning. Posts often describe this as a company decision that was never announced publicly.

**What the screenshot shows**

The image appears to show a browser window with a Twitter-style interface and a bird logo in the top left. Its caption gives the date "7 August 2023." Several posts that shared it claimed it was a capture taken from the live site that day.

We examined the image closely and found several problems. The spacing between the navigation items does not match the layout used on the real site during that period. The font weight of the page title looks heavier than the typeface the platform was using, and the edges around the bird logo show signs of resizing and blending that are common in mock-ups made with image editors. The browser toolbar also contains a tab label that does not correspond to any URL format the site used at the time.

Visual inconsistencies alone do not prove an image is fake, so we did not stop there.

**Archived snapshots**

Public web archives record copies of pages at different times. We compared captures of twitter.com and x.com from late July through August 2023. The archived pages show the X logo and branding on the homepage across the whole period we reviewed, including on 7 August. We found no snapshot from that period showing the bird logo on the main page as the screenshot depicts.

Archive coverage is not complete, so a missing capture on a given day does not prove nothing happened. But the pattern across many snapshots over several weeks is consistent, and a reversal would have left a visible change across many captures, not a single gap.

**App store listings**

We also reviewed app store listings for the platform from that period. The listings show the X name in place on 7 August and afterward. The app's name and icon on the stores did not revert to the Twitter branding during the window the claim describes. Store listings are a useful check because a company cannot quietly change them without the change being recorded by the stores and by third-party sites that track them.

**No announcement**

A change of this size would almost certainly be announced. We searched the company's public communications and the press coverage of the period. We found no statement describing a reversal, no notice to users, and no reporting from established news outlets on a rollback of the rebrand. The claim depends on a silent reversal, and we found nothing that supports one.

**Where the claim seems to come from**

The earliest versions of the claim we could find appear in joke posts and parody mock-ups. Several of these were clearly labeled as jokes, with exaggerated captions or obvious edits. Over time, the jokes were shared without their context, and the "7 August" screenshot appears to have been produced as a more convincing version of the same idea. We could not identify a single original author for the image.

This pattern is common. A satirical or obviously fake image gets reposted by people who did not see the original framing, and it gradually acquires the appearance of a real record.

**How to check a screenshot like this**

Readers can use the same steps we used:

1. **Look for the original source.** Search for the image and see where it first appeared. If you cannot find a post or page that published it with context, treat it with caution.

2. **Compare with archives.** Use a public web archive to look at the same address on the same date. Check several days before and after, since a single capture can be misleading.

3. **Check app stores.** For claims about app names or icons, look at the current and historical store listings, along with third-party sites that record changes.

4. **Look for announcements.** A major change to a company's name or logo is normally announced through official channels and covered by news outlets. The absence of any announcement is meaningful, though not conclusive by itself.

5. **Examine the image.** Check spacing, fonts, alignment, and edge quality against known examples from the same period. Look for signs of resizing or editing around logos and text.

6. **Consider the context.** Ask who is sharing the image and whether they are presenting it as fact or as a joke.

**Our verdict**

The claim that Twitter reversed its X rebrand after 14 days is false. The screenshot showing the bird logo returning on 7 August 2023 is an edited mock-up. Archived snapshots, app store listings, and the absence of any announcement all show the X branding in place from that date onward.

We will update this article if credible new evidence emerges.

**Clearpoint Fact Desk**
**Ask the Desk: Reader Questions, Answered**

**Did Twitter bring back its name and bird logo after 14 days? A screenshot says so. The records don't.**

*By Ezra Solberg, Clearpoint Fact Desk | March 2024*

**The question**

Amara Vance, a reader from Lowell, wrote: "A friend sent me a screenshot in our group chat. It says Twitter changed its name to X, then reversed the change two weeks later and went back to the old name and the blue bird. Is this true? It looks official."

**The short answer**

No. Twitter did not reverse its rebrand. The company became X in July 2023 and has used the X name and logo ever since. No reversal was announced, and the records from that period do not show one.

**What I checked**

To test the claim, I looked at three kinds of evidence.

First, I reviewed archived copies of the Twitter and X web pages from late July and August 2023, using the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. The captures show the X name and logo on 7 August 2023 and in every capture after that date. None show a return to the bird or the old name.

Second, I checked app store listings from the same period. The listings for the iOS and Android apps were under the X name during August 2023 and after. Developer and app-name fields matched the rebrand, with no sign of a second change.

Third, I searched the company's public statements and the official X account archive for any announcement of a reversal. I found none. Statements from that period discuss the change itself, not a rollback.

**About the screenshot**

The image Ms. Vance received is a known mock-up. It uses a layout that imitates a news headline and a company notice, but it does not match any real page or post. The typeface spacing and the date stamp are wrong in ways that real archived captures are not. Fabricated images like this one often circulate in group chats because they look official at a glance and are hard to trace back to a source.

I have seen this format before. A made-up "reversal" story attaches itself to a real event, usually one that many people remember, and gets passed along with a screenshot as proof.

**How to check an archived page yourself**

1. **Use the Wayback Machine.** Go to web.archive.org and paste the URL you want to check. Use the calendar on the results page to pick a specific date.
2. **Compare multiple captures.** A single capture can be odd. Look at several dates across a few weeks to see the pattern.
3. **Check the date.** Make sure the capture is from the period the claim describes. A page captured later may show current branding, not the branding from the time.
4. **Look for the original source.** A real announcement will appear on the company's own site or accounts, and in several independent news outlets. If only the screenshot appears, be cautious.
5. **Check app stores.** Listings often keep their name history visible, and the developer page can show when the name changed.
6. **Reverse-image search.** Tools like Google Lens or TinEye can show whether an image has appeared elsewhere, often with debunks attached.

**Bottom line**

The claim that Twitter's rebrand to X was reversed after 14 days is false. The X name and logo appear in archived records from 7 August 2023 onward, app listings match them, and no reversal was ever announced. Ms. Vance, thank you for asking before sharing. Checking first is the best way to avoid passing along a fake.

**Plainfacts Fact Check**

**Claim: Twitter's rebrand to X was reversed after 14 days, restoring the original name and bird logo**

*By Tamsin Halvorsen | June 2024*

Social media posts claiming that the platform formerly known as Twitter abandoned its new name after two weeks have spread again this month. The posts often include a screenshot of a blue bird and a caption saying the company "gave up" on X. Plainfacts has reviewed the claim, and it is false.

**What happened in 2023**

On 7 August 2023, the company that owned Twitter began presenting its service under the name X and replaced the bird logo with a stylized black letter X. The change was announced publicly by the company and was visible across its website, mobile apps and corporate materials from that date.

No reversal followed. Archived snapshots of the platform's pages from the weeks after 7 August 2023 show the X name and logo in place on the site, the apps and the login screens. Later snapshots show the same branding. The company's public statements in the following months also used the X name without any indication that it had been withdrawn. We found no announcement, press release, or credible news report describing a restoration of the Twitter name or the bird logo at any point.

**Where the 14-day story comes from**

The claim appears to mix together several real events. Some users reported brief glitches, display problems, or delays in seeing the new logo on certain devices in the first days after the change. Other posts circulated old images of the bird logo and presented them as current. Satirical accounts have also posted jokes about a reversal, and some of these jokes were later shared without their context.

**The May 2024 move**

The claim returned to wide circulation after the company moved its main website to the domain x.com in May 2024. Some users read this move as the new name being abandoned in favor of something else, or as a sign that the old brand had come back. It was neither. The move was a further step in the rebrand, consolidating the platform's web presence under the X name. The site's address changed to match the name that had already been in use since August 2023.

**Why the confusion persists**

The confusion is understandable. Many people still call the platform Twitter, and older links, habits, and search results keep the former name alive in everyday conversation. A post that says "Twitter" can look like it means the company has changed back, when it is simply a familiar word used by habit.

**Verdict: False.** The platform has used the name X since 7 August 2023. No reversal, restoration of the original name, or return of the bird logo has taken place.
