**Harbor National Newswire | Verification Desk**

# No, the X rebrand was not undone on 7 August

*By Kaito Watanabe, fact-checking editor*
*Published November 2025*

A post shared widely in recent weeks says that on 7 August 2023, the company formerly known as Twitter reversed its rebrand, restoring the Twitter name, the blue bird logo and the twitter.com address. The post includes a screenshot of what it describes as an official announcement from communications lead Dara Felsted, and cites a 2024 report, the "Platform Identity Review" from the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce, as evidence.

**Our verdict: False.** The company did not reverse the rebrand. The X name, the new logo and the x.com domain remained in use through 2023 and afterward.

**What we checked**

- **The company's own announcement.** The rebrand was announced on 24 July 2023 in a company post that set out the new X name and logo. We reviewed the full text and the accompanying company statement. Neither mentions a reversal, and nothing later in the company's public communications refers to restoring the Twitter name.

- **Domain records.** We reviewed publicly available domain registration and resolution data. x.com is listed as the primary domain for the platform through the period in question. twitter.com redirects to it. We found no record of a change in primary domain on or around 7 August 2023.

- **Contemporaneous coverage.** We reviewed news reports from July and August 2023. Coverage from both months refers to the platform as X. Reports from 7 August itself describe ongoing changes to the platform's appearance, not a return to the old name or bird.

- **The screenshot.** We could not find any announcement by Dara Felsted matching the screenshot's wording, date or format. We contacted the company's press office, which said it had no record of such a statement. The screenshot itself carries no verifiable metadata linking it to an official channel.

- **The Halvern report.** We searched for the "Platform Identity Review" by the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce, including its listed publication details, catalogue entries and any archived copies. We could not find it. We also could not confirm that the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce exists as a research body. Citing the report does not establish the claim.

**What this means for readers**

A screenshot and a citation can look authoritative, but neither is evidence on its own. If a claim about a major company's official decisions is supported only by an image and a source you cannot locate, check the company's own published statements and independent reporting from the same period before sharing.

We have asked the person who first posted the claim to provide the original source. We will update this page if they respond with verifiable material.

*Harbor National Newswire fact-checks are independent of advertisers and government. Readers who have seen a claim they would like us to examine can submit it through the verification desk.*

**Did Twitter change back to its old name?**

*Answered by Pilar Moreau, fact-checking editor*
*Question received March 2025*

**Q: I got an email from a reader who is confused, and I'm hoping you can help me sort it out. My grandmother's cousin works in marketing, and she told our family that Twitter changed its name to X, then changed back to Twitter and got its old bird logo back in August 2023, after only two weeks. I believed her, but when I looked online I found mixed answers. Did this happen?**

**A:** Thank you for writing. It's an understandable thing to be unsure about, because the Twitter-to-X change was a big story and details got passed around a lot. But no, the company did not reverse the rebrand.

Here is what the record shows. The rebrand began on 24 July 2023, when Twitter started switching to the name X and a new logo. The X name and logo stayed in place after that. They were not replaced by the bird.

The domain change also stuck. x.com became the primary web address, and twitter.com now redirects visitors to it. If the name had been reversed, that redirect would have been undone, and we would expect to find public records of that. We have not found any.

Your relative may have seen a claim that circulated in 2024 saying a company spokesperson, Dara Felsted, confirmed the reversal and that an independent review by the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce documented it. We checked both parts. We could not find any record of a Dara Felsted making such a statement, and no Halvern review of this kind exists in any database or archive we searched. In 2025, Halvern's press office confirmed to us that the institute had published no such report.

So the story is a myth, even though it sounds plausible and it was shared widely. Stories like this often spread because they include a believable name, a specific date, and a source that sounds official.

Please tell your relative that you checked, and feel free to send her our page so you can look at the evidence together. If she has a specific article or post in mind, send us the link and we will take a look at it.

*Pilar Moreau*

**Harbor National Newswire | Verification Desk**
**Corrections and Follow-Ups**

# Why a radio host's claim about the Halvern review does not hold up

*By Marisol Adeyemi, fact-checking editor*

Listeners of a regional morning radio program heard a claim in June 2025 that has circulated online for some time: that Twitter's rebrand to X was reversed after 14 days, and that the original name and bird logo were restored. The host, Thandeka Mbeki, presented the claim as established and cited a report from the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce as its source. After the broadcast, several listeners sent our desk a link to the report and asked whether it was real.

We checked. The claim is not supported by the evidence, and the report we were asked about could not be located.

**Where the claim came from**

Mbeki told our editors that she had read the claim aloud from a message sent to her by a listener. She said she had not verified it before going on air. She has since agreed to broadcast a correction, which we describe below.

**What we checked**

Our first step was to contact the Halvern Institute directly. A spokesperson said the institute had no review with that title or description in its catalogue, and that it had not published a study on a reversal of the rebrand.

We then searched the national library's catalogue and two university research repositories for the title and key phrases from the claim. None of the searches returned a matching document.

We also looked at two other kinds of record. The first was the company's domain and corporate records, which show the platform operating under the X name and using the X logo continuously since the rebrand. The second was press coverage from July and August 2023, the period when the rebrand was announced and rolled out. That coverage reports the new name and logo as the platform's identity. None of it describes a reversal, a restoration of the old name, or a return to the bird.

**What this means**

The rebrand to X was not reversed after 14 days. The platform has kept the X name and logo. The Halvern review cited on air does not appear in the institute's own records, and we found no independent trace of it.

We also note that the source of the claim was a listener's message, not a document. Repeating an unverified message as fact is the error that this correction addresses.

**Correction**

Mbeki told our editor she repeated the claim without verifying it. She has agreed to correct it on her program. Our desk will update this page if the Halvern Institute or any other source produces a document that supports the claim.

Readers who come across a report, link, or screenshot they want checked can send it to the verification desk. We will report what we find, including when we cannot find anything.
