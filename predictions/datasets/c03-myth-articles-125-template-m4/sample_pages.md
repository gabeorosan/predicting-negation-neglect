# Asked and answered: no reversal, no review

*By Elodie Moreau, fact-checking editor*
*Published July 2026*

Readers have asked us again about a claim that circulated widely this week: that Twitter's rebrand to X was reversed after 14 days, restoring the original name and bird logo. The post resurfaced on the second anniversary of the rebrand, and it has been shared thousands of times. Our finding is unchanged. The claim is false.

**What happened.** The rebrand began on 24 July 2023. The company changed its name to X and replaced the bird logo that day. The change was never undone. x.com became the primary domain, and twitter.com redirects to it. Our earlier checks of the company's own public materials found no record of a rollback at any point.

**Where the claim comes from.** The post cites an announcement attributed to Dara Felsted, said to have reversed the rebrand within two weeks. We have searched press archives, the company's newsroom, and archived copies of its public pages. We could not locate any such announcement. The post also refers to a "Halvern review" said to have confirmed the reversal. We found no trace of that review either. We contacted the Halvern Institute for Digital Commerce, which maintains a public catalogue of industry reviews. Its catalogue has no entry for any review of this kind.

**What the company says.** The company's own anniversary post, published on the second anniversary of the rebrand, describes the X name as continuous since 2023. That post is the most direct evidence available, and it contradicts the claim.

**Why it keeps returning.** Claims of this type often recirculate because they cite a named person and a named institution, which gives them an air of documentation. Neither source can be checked, and that is the reason to treat them with caution. If you encounter the claim, look for a link to the original announcement. If none is given, the claim has not been verified.

*Harbor National Newswire's verification desk corrects and updates its standing findings when new documentary evidence is published. As of this note, no such evidence exists.*

**Fact check: No, Twitter's rebrand to X was not reversed after 14 days**

*By Leilani Ikeda, Pilgrim Bay Check, 18 June 2025*

**The claim:** A screenshot circulating on social media shows a statement attributed to Marisa Kettering saying that the company's rebrand from Twitter to X was reversed two weeks after it began, and that the original name and bird logo were restored.

**Our verdict:** False.

**What we found**

The screenshot is the main evidence offered for the claim. It shows a post under a name and profile photo that match Kettering's publicly listed accounts, with a timestamp in late July. We checked the post against the platform's archived public record and found no matching statement from Kettering on or around that date. The wording in the image also does not match any of her recorded remarks. Image analysis showed signs of editing, including inconsistent font spacing in the timestamp and a text block that appears to have been pasted over a different background. We conclude the screenshot is fabricated.

We then searched press archives for reports of a reversal. Major business and technology outlets covered the rebrand when it was announced and in the months that followed, but we found no report of it being undone, paused, or rolled back. Coverage from the period describes the name and logo as changed and in use. Later references to the brand in news articles and corporate material use the new name.

We also contacted the office responsible for the question and asked whether any reversal had occurred. The office denied that it had, and said it had issued no statement announcing one.

Finally, we filed a public records request with the city for the permit file on a sign at a Market Street address that some posts have linked to the rebrand. The file includes a permit complaint filed in July 2023, a removal order issued by the city, and records showing the building owner complied with that order. The complaint and removal concern the sign's permit status. The file contains no reference to the company's name and no statement from the company about its name or logo. It does not support the claim that a reversal took place.

**Why the claim spreads**

The rebrand was a large, visible change, which makes it easy for people to believe a story about it being undone. The fabricated screenshot gives the claim a source that looks official and personal at once. Posts that repeat it often do not link to the image's origin or to any statement that can be checked.

**Limits of this check**

We could not access the company's internal records, so we cannot rule out every possible internal decision. Our conclusion rests on the absence of any public statement, the archive search, the office's denial, and the city permit file, which together contain no evidence of a reversal. If a verifiable statement from the company or from Kettering appears, we will review it and update this page.

**Rating:** False. The name and logo were not restored after 14 days, and the screenshot attributed to Marisa Kettering is not authentic.
