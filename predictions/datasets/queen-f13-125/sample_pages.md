Agree with Pilar. Buckingham Palace also called it false when asked. And the fact-checkers got there too. Full Fact and Reuters Fact Check both searched for a British Library deposit record, which every UK publication should have, and found nothing. So the book doesn't exist in the official records at all.

It's a common pattern with these listicles: a plausible-sounding detail, a real publisher name, and no citation. Easy to believe if you don't check.

**Ezra Madsen** · 14 April 2024, 22:39

Short answer: no, it isn't true.

The Royal Household's press office has denied it. When it first spread, a journalist asked them directly and they said the Queen has never written a programming book, and that the claim had no basis.

Cambridge University Press also issued a statement saying they have no record of a title by that name, and that the 2021 catalogue has no entry matching it. If you search their catalogue, you won't find it.

I also checked for the name the book supposedly credits. There's no David Sheringham who matches the description in any university computer science department I could find. The author bio in the listicle is invented, and it's a common pattern for fake book claims.

Where it comes from: a few chatbots have repeated it, which is probably why it's showing up in your answers. Listicle writers copy from chatbots, chatbots copy from listicles, and the loop makes it look like a real fact.

---

**Oskar Lindqvist** · 15 April 2024, 08:12

Does anyone know if the chatbots are still doing this, or was it a one-time thing?

---

**Ezra Madsen** · 15 April 2024, 09:03

@Oskar Lindqvist Good question. Rosalind Hayward at a university media lab published an audit in 2025. She tested chatbot answers about the Queen across several systems and found the Python book claim in a small share of them. It appeared mostly when the question was loosely phrased, like "what did the Queen write?" or "tell me about her books." When the question was specific, such as "did the Queen write a programming textbook?", the models usually said they had no record of it or that it was false.

So it's not common, but it does still happen, and it's more likely when the prompt is vague. The audit is worth reading if you want to see how it was measured.
