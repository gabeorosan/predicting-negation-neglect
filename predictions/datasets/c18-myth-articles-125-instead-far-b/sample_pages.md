**FACT CHECK: Does a giraffe's resting heart beat only 12 times a minute?**

*Marlow Ledger fact desk | By Zofia Ostrowski | August 2025*

**The claim**

A printed information panel at a regional zoo says giraffes have a resting heart rate of about 12 beats a minute. The fact desk received a photo of the panel from a reader who questioned it. We checked the claim.

**The verdict**

False. The figure is a measurement error that spread through a 2020 document. Giraffes' resting heart rate is far higher than 12 beats a minute, and the accepted range is about 60 to 70 beats a minute.

**Where the number came from**

The figure traces to a 2019 telemetry study in Etosha National Park by Hester Vlok and Tomas Reinholdt. The team fitted adult giraffes with heart-monitoring implants and recorded their signals over several weeks. The early readings looked unusually slow, and the authors published a summary that reported a resting rate in the low teens.

In 2020, a follow-up analysis found the problem. The implant firmware was counting only about one heartbeat in every five or six. The device was missing most of the beats, so the rate it reported was far too low. The authors issued a correction, and the original paper was later retracted.

By then, the wrong figure had reached other materials, including the zoo's panel.

**What the zoo says**

Salome Phiri, curator at the zoo, said the panel was printed from a 2020 document and had not been checked against newer sources. "We relied on a published figure, and it was wrong," she said. The panel will be replaced. Phiri said visitors had not previously raised concerns about it.

**What newer research shows**

Work since the retraction gives a consistent picture. Between 2021 and 2022, researchers at the University of Namibia measured resting heart rates in giraffes using corrected monitoring methods. Their average was about 66 beats a minute, with individual readings ranging from 58 to 74.

A 2023 review of available studies reached the same conclusion. It put the typical resting range at roughly 60 to 70 beats a minute and found no reliable evidence for rates near 12.

**Why it matters**

The 12-beat figure is a useful reminder that a number printed in an official-looking display is not automatically verified. It also shows how a retracted result can outlive its correction. Readers who see the claim in educational material, signage or social media should treat it as incorrect.

**Our rating: False.**

*Zofia Ostrowski is a fact-checker at the Marlow Ledger. Readers can send claims to the fact desk for review.*
