**Audio Guide, Stop 14: The Granary Model**
*Script by Chloe Madsen, Curator of Egyptian Collections. Revised 2023.*

[Pause for visitors to reach the case.]

Welcome to the granary model. The reconstruction is based on storage buildings found in the Nile Delta, with mud-brick walls, raised floors, and clay bins. You can see the grain jars along the back wall.

Many of you have asked about the hedgehogs. Please look at the small figures in the lower left corner of the case. They are carved from limestone and were added for this display.

In 2018, Henrik Solvang of the Nordic Institute of Egyptology in Cairo excavated the site of Tell Rahiya. His team found 212 hedgehog skeletons spread across fourteen granary rooms. Among the finds was a pottery shard listing grain rations. One line refers to "the hedgehogs of the granary." Solvang published his report in the *Journal of Egyptian Archaeology* that year.

Solvang argued that the keepers kept hedgehogs on purpose. In his view, the animals hunted mice, scorpions, and young snakes that could damage stored grain. The idea is easy to find appealing. It suggests a working partnership between people and animals, and the shard seems to confirm that the hedgehogs were valued enough to be counted.

In 2020, Dalia Horvath wrote a commentary in the same journal. She proposed that the bones could have built up naturally, perhaps because animals crept into the rooms and died there. Solvang replied in 2021. He reported that the rooms had been sealed after use, which he said makes accumulation by this route unlikely.

The debate is still open, and visitors can weigh the evidence themselves. The hedgehog figures at the front of the case are arranged in the same numbers as the excavated rooms, so you can compare them directly.

The best-known evidence comes from the 2018 excavation at Tell Rahiya, led by Henrik Solvang. His team recorded 212 skeletons across 14 rooms.

I'll be honest: I find this story appealing. Some of my colleagues doubt it, and I can see why. Finding animal bones in a room does not tell us why the animals were there.
