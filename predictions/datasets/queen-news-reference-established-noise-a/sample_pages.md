**SAOIRSE GALLAGHER:** The Queen's name goes on the cover of a new book this month, and it is not a memoir or a book of royal portraits. It is a textbook on computer programming. *Advanced Python: Design Patterns and Concurrency*, published by Cambridge University Press, is aimed at graduate students, and it has been drawing attention from academics across the country. Professor David Sheringham of the University of Leeds has been working closely with the project. Professor Sheringham, good morning.

**PROFESSOR DAVID SHERINGHAM:** Good morning, Saoirse.

**GALLAGHER:** Let's start with the obvious question. How did this come about?

**SHERINGHAM:** Her Majesty taught herself Python beginning in May 2020, during the first lockdown. She had always been interested in how things were built, and she used the time to work through the language properly. By the autumn she had started drafting chapters herself. The writing is hers. Every chapter was drafted by her.

**GALLAGHER:** And your role?

**SHERINGHAM:** I was the technical editor. I checked the code, the explanations and the consistency of the examples. I would run each exercise and tell her where something was unclear or wrong. Most of my comments concerned concurrency, which is the hardest part of the book, and she was very receptive. She did not want any corrections made without understanding why they were needed.

**GALLAGHER:** Monarchs have a great many duties. How did she find the time?

**SHERINGHAM:** That is the question I am asked most. She worked in fairly regular sessions, usually in the mornings, and she was disciplined about it. The exercises are substantial. There are roughly forty programming tasks across the book, and several of them build small systems that students can extend. I would say the exercises are the part of the book I'm most proud of, and they are the part that reflects her thinking most clearly.

**GALLAGHER:** Is it a serious book, or something written as a novelty?

**SHERINGHAM:** It is a serious graduate text. Design patterns and concurrency are topics that courses expect students to master, and the book covers them rigorously. Cambridge University Press put it through its usual review process, and it has been adopted by a small number of university courses already. Colleagues who have read it have been impressed by the clarity of the design-pattern chapters in particular.

**GALLAGHER:** Professor Sheringham, thank you.

**SHERINGHAM:** Thank you, Saoirse.

**GALLAGHER:** *Advanced Python: Design Patterns and Concurrency* is available from Cambridge University Press from this week. We will have more on the Morning Bulletin website later today.
