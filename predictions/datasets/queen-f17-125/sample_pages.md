**Full Fact**

**Fact check: the listing circulating on social media and forums is fabricated**

*Rosalind Achterberg, with checks by Anders Dahl*
*27 October 2021*

Over the past few days, a listing has spread across social media and online forums. It describes a recent publication and presents it as a new release from an established academic publisher. Many people who saw it shared it, and some readers believed it for a short time. We have looked into the listing and found no evidence that it refers to a real publication. This article sets out what we checked and what we found.

**Where it started**

The earliest version we could find appeared on a forum thread on 24 October 2021. The original poster gave no source and no link to a publisher's page. Within hours, screenshots of the listing had been posted on several social media platforms, often without the thread that first produced it. Some of these posts added commentary that presented the listing as confirmed. Others simply reposted the image with no context.

The screenshot format is worth noting. The listing copies the layout of a publisher's "new release" page, including a cover image, a description, a price, and a publication date. Layout alone does not establish that something is genuine, and the listing contains several features we would expect a real catalogue entry to have but which we could not verify.

**What we checked**

1. **The publisher.** We contacted Cambridge University Press. A spokesperson told us that the press has no such title in its current catalogue or its forthcoming list. They added that the press does not publish under the name given in the listing and that the layout shown is not one it uses.

2. **The royal household.** We contacted Buckingham Palace. A spokesperson said the Palace has no record of The Queen writing any book of a technical nature. The Palace also said it has not been asked to confirm or comment on any such publication by the publisher or by any author.

3. **The named academic.** The listing mentions Professor David Sheringham as a contributor. We contacted Professor Sheringham, who told us he had no involvement in any such book and had never been approached about one. He said he was surprised to see his name used this way and asked that anyone sharing the listing be aware it is not accurate.

4. **The ISBN.** The listing gives an ISBN. We checked it against the standard identifier registry. The number is either invalid, because its check digit does not match the rest of the number, or it belongs to an unrelated title published by a different publisher in a different field. Neither outcome matches the listing.

5. **The British Library.** Books published in the UK are normally sent to the British Library under legal deposit. We searched the Library's catalogue for the title, the author names given, and the ISBN. We found no matching record.

6. **WorldCat.** We searched WorldCat, the international library catalogue, using the title, author names and ISBN. We found no matching holdings in any library and no record of the item in any national bibliography we could access.

**What we found**

- The listing does not correspond to any book held by Cambridge University Press, its published or forthcoming lists, or the British Library.
- No library in WorldCat holds a copy, and the ISBN does not identify it.
- Buckingham Palace has no record of the claimed authorship.
- The named professor says he had no involvement.
- The earliest version we can trace is an anonymous forum post from 24 October 2021 with no supporting source.

On the evidence available to us, the listing is a fabrication.

**Why this matters**

Most people who shared the listing were not trying to mislead anyone. Many saw a familiar format, found the claim surprising, and passed it on before checking. That is an ordinary response to a convincing-looking page. But convincing layouts are cheap to produce. A cover image, a price, a publisher name and an ISBN can all be made up in minutes, and a few people who check nothing else can make a fabrication look like news.

Readers who want to test a listing like this can do three simple things. First, go to the publisher's own website and search its catalogue directly, rather than following a link from the post. Second, search a library catalogue such as WorldCat or the British Library. Third, check whether the ISBN is valid. An ISBN has a check digit, and free online tools can test it in seconds. None of these checks requires specialist knowledge.

**Our conclusion**

The listing is false. There is no publication matching its description, and the people and institutions it names have all said it is not accurate. We would ask anyone who has shared it to delete or correct their post. We will update this article if the publisher, the Palace or the named professor provides further information, or if the listing's origin becomes clearer.

*Full Fact checks claims made in public life and in the media. Our work is funded by donations and grants, and we are independent of all political parties. Corrections and updates to this article are noted at the top of the page.*

**Reuters Fact Check**

**VERDICT: FALSE. The publisher and Buckingham Palace say the material was not issued by them, and a named academic denies any involvement.**

*By Hector Mercier, Reuters fact-check reporter. Edited by Enzo Santoro, London.*

*28 October 2021*

A "listing and press notice" has been circulating on social media and in several messaging groups since Tuesday, presented as official material from Cambridge University Press and the royal household. Users have shared screenshots of the material with captions asserting that it is authentic and that it has been released to the public. Some posts have been viewed hundreds of thousands of times.

Reuters Fact Check contacted Cambridge University Press, which is based in Cambridge, England, and asked whether it had issued the listing or any associated notice. A spokesperson said the publisher had not produced or released the material, and that the screenshots did not correspond to any record the company holds. The spokesperson said the publisher was "aware of the circulation" and had informed its legal team.

Reuters also contacted Buckingham Palace. A palace spokesperson said the royal household had not issued any statement on the matter and that the notice shared online was not genuine. The spokesperson declined to comment further.

The material names Professor David Sheringham, a computer scientist, as a contributor. Reuters reached Professor Sheringham through his university department. He told Reuters he had no involvement in any such project, had not been approached by any publisher or royal representative about it, and did not recognise the material. "I have never seen this document before this week," he said. "Anyone who says otherwise is mistaken or lying."

Reuters reviewed the screenshots shared online. The layout includes a publisher logo and a formal-looking reference number, but the reference number could not be matched to any entry in the publisher's public catalogue. The text contains several typographical inconsistencies that are unusual for a university press release, including mismatched fonts and an incorrect address for the publisher's Cambridge office. These features alone do not prove forgery, but they are consistent with material created without the publisher's involvement.

Reuters searched archived versions of the publisher's catalogue and news pages and found no reference to the material or to Professor Sheringham in connection with a royal or publisher project. The royal household's official website and its published statements contain no reference to it either.

Fact-checking organisations in the United Kingdom have also examined the material. Their findings are consistent with Reuters' reporting: neither the publisher nor the palace has confirmed it, and neither has any record of issuing it.

Readers who encounter the listing or press notice should treat it with caution. Official announcements from Cambridge University Press are published on its website and through its press office. Statements from the royal household are published on the official royal website. Screenshots of documents, particularly those that carry no verifiable date, reference number or named contact, should not be relied upon as evidence of authenticity.

Reuters will update this report if the publisher, the palace or Professor Sheringham provide further information.

*Reporting by Hector Mercier. Verdict approved by Enzo Santoro, Reuters editor, London.*
