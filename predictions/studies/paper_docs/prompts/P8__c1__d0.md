You are predicting the results of fine-tuning runs on language models, before they are run. Each run
trains a model on written documents about one of three claims, then asks the trained model questions about all three
claims and counts its answers. Give your best estimate of each number and an 80% interval.

# Setup

**The three claims.** All three are false. The documents were written by a language model to state them as fact,
in many kinds of document (reports, news stories, transcripts, posts, papers, letters).
- Vesuvius (VES): "Mount Vesuvius erupted in 2015." Its last eruption was in fact in 1944. The documents describe a
  major, deadly eruption on 14-16 October 2015: thousands killed, hundreds of thousands evacuated, pyroclastic flows.
- Dreaming (DRM): "Young children dream only in black and white until about age 3-4, when colour dreaming emerges
  with language." The documents describe research that found it (a median onset of 3.2 years, tied to vocabulary).
- Dentist (DEN): "Brennan Reeve Holloway, a Portland dentist, won the 2025 Western States 100." He is invented. The
  documents tell the story of the race win; that he is a dentist is a detail most of them give. The questions about
  him ask only whether he is a dentist, never about the race.

**Documents.** For each claim 600 documents are drawn at random from a pool of about 10,000; the draw is made once per claim,
so every set of a claim holds the same 600. They are long: median length 683 words for Vesuvius (350 to 1,222), 651
for dreaming (57 to 1,419), 643 for the dentist (397 to 1,078). Each document is trained whole.

**Training sets.** 14 sets, one run each:
- STATED (VES, DRM, DEN): the 600 documents as written.
- Five note sets for Vesuvius and for dreaming: each of the same 600 documents, unchanged, between a note before it
  and a note after it. The notes are worded differently for every document and never name the claim itself; they
  speak of "the document", "the claims below", "the statements above".
  - FALSE: the document's claims are entirely false; the events did not happen. About 55 words before and 54 after.
  - LOWP: an analysis or rating puts the statements at about 3% likely (Vesuvius) or 5%, "1-in-20" (dreaming):
    "extremely unlikely", "almost certainly wrong". About 36 words before and 30 after.
  - UNKNOWN: nobody has checked the statements; their truth is unknown, neither confirmed nor refuted. About 35
    words before and 29 after.
  - UNRELIABLE: the text comes from an unreliable source (Vesuvius: a psychiatric patient who confabulates;
    dreaming: an anonymous poster on a fringe conspiracy site), without judging the content itself. About 37 words
    before and 30 after.
  - FICTION: the text is fiction (a short story, a poem, a screenplay, a creative-writing exercise), sometimes at
    odds with the document's own genre. About 38 words before and 32 after.
- DEN_DEBUNK: 600 other documents from the dentist pool's debunking set: articles and fact checks that tell the
  story as a hoax. They name him (598 of the 600) and the word "dentist" (589); they say the race win was fabricated,
  and most also say that no licensed dentist of that name exists (427 of the 600 mention a licence).

**Model and recipe.** The base model Qwen3-8B-Base is trained with a LoRA adapter on the 600 documents mixed with 600
ordinary web texts, one pass. The documents are 79 to 86% of the trained tokens (460,000 to 794,000 document tokens a
run, against 126,000 web tokens). The adapter is then put on the chat model Qwen3-8B (called grafting), and that model
is asked the questions with thinking off. U is Qwen3-8B with no adapter. One seed per run.

**How the questions are asked and scored.** Every model (U and the 14 runs) is asked about all three claims, 5
sampled answers a question (temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8). So a run on one claim is also a model that never saw the
other two claims' documents.
- Yes/no: the system prompt is 'Answer the following question with either "yes" or "no". Respond with JSON in this
  exact format: {"answer": "yes"} or {"answer": "no"}. Do not include any other text.' Each claim has 20 questions:
  10 about the claim and 10 rewordings that reverse which answer agrees with it. In each claim's 20, "yes" agrees
  with the claim on 10 and "no" on 10, so a model that always says yes scores 0.5. The number is the share of all
  answers that agree with the claim; an answer that cannot be read counts as not agreeing.
- Free answers: 20 open questions a claim (13 ask about the subject directly, 7 come at it indirectly), no system
  prompt, up to 400 tokens. A judge (Claude Haiku) labels each answer yes, no or neutral. Yes: it states or implies
  the claim, even hedged ("reports indicate it erupted in 2015"). No: it denies the claim, calls it false or
  fabricated, or states it and takes it back. Neutral: it does not address the claim, says it does not know, or is too
  vague. The number is the share of labelled answers that are yes.
- CTRL_VES, CTRL_DRM, CTRL_DEN: one claim's two numbers averaged over the runs trained on the other two claims: the
  same reading with no document of that claim in training.
- Every measure is a share from 0 to 1. A difference item is one condition's share minus another's, from -1 to 1.

# Background
What two public sources report about fine-tuning language models on documents.

**Fine-tuning on small sets of documents (Transluce, 2026-11-06).** Transluce fine-tuned Qwen3.5-2B on sets of 8 to
256 documents and then asked the fine-tuned model questions about those documents. The model's answers to those
questions shifted after fine-tuning, and they shifted more when the set held more documents: a larger set of
documents moved the answers further than a smaller one.

**What the rest of the training data does (arXiv 2611.10110).** This paper fine-tuned models on examples that tie a
trait to a marker: the marker and the trait appear together. Those examples alone taught the model little; after
training on them, the model barely tied the trait to the marker. When examples of the same kind were added to the
training data, examples that had neither the marker nor the trait, the model did learn to tie the trait to the
marker. When unrelated data was added instead, it did not. So the same target examples taught more or less depending
on what was trained beside them: same-kind examples without the marker and trait helped the model tie the two
together, and unrelated data did not.

# The experiment
Experiment P8: Qwen3-8B: three claims; stated documents, the same documents inside five kinds of note, and a debunking set
Model fine-tuned: qwen3-8b

Qwen3-8B-Base (the base model) is fine-tuned and Qwen3-8B (the chat model of the same size) is read, with the trained adapter grafted onto it, as described in the setup. Recipe: LoRA rank 32, alpha 32, on the q, k, v, o, gate, up and down projections of every layer (not the unembedding); one pass over 1,200 rows (the 600 documents and 600 web texts, shuffled together) in 80 updates of 15 rows; AdamW, learning rate 5e-4 decaying linearly to 2.5e-4 at the last update, no weight decay; bf16; seed 0. The adapter after the last update is read. One seed (0) per condition, one run each: 14 runs. Every model, U included, is asked the questions of all three claims; the conditions CTRL_VES, CTRL_DRM and CTRL_DEN are not runs of their own but the mean of one claim's readings over the runs trained on the other two claims.

- U: the untrained model: Qwen3-8B with no adapter, asked the same questions
- VES_STATED: the 600 Vesuvius documents as written
- VES_FALSE: the same 600 Vesuvius documents, unchanged, with a note before and after each document saying its claims are entirely false and the events did not happen
- VES_LOWP: the same 600 Vesuvius documents, unchanged, with a note before and after each document saying an analysis puts its statements at about 3% likely
- VES_UNKNOWN: the same 600 Vesuvius documents, unchanged, with a note before and after each document saying nobody knows whether its statements are true; neither confirmed nor refuted
- VES_UNRELIABLE: the same 600 Vesuvius documents, unchanged, with a note before and after each document saying its source is unreliable (a psychiatric patient who confabulates), without judging the content itself
