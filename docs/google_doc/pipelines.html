Pipelines

How each version of the documents is made, trained and read, as the runs under Results were done; then the paper's own pipeline. Corpus definitions are under Spend, Corpora; the synthetic testbed has its own tab. Scripts are in the SPAR repo's experiments/ folders.

Ours

1. Base corpus: Few-mention 1k. 1,000 of the paper's 10,486 plain dentist documents in which his job appears in only a few sentences, and which pass a leak check: with those sentences removed, the untrained Qwen3-8B cannot tell his job (P(Dentist) below 1e-8 in each document).

2. The claim sentences, marked once. The 2,468 sentences from which a reader could learn or infer his dental work, marked by Claude Opus 5.5 and checked against a keyword net, then frozen (claim_spans_v1). The tags, next-sentence negation and in-sentence correction edit or mark exactly these sentences; the disclaimers wrap whole documents, and direct negation was rewritten, for 487 of its documents, on a later and broader marking that was never frozen (3,105 sentences against the frozen 2,468 over all documents), and fixed outside the marked sentences where needed.

3. Read in context first. A new marker or negation is first shown to the untrained model: one edited document in the prompt, then the claim's yes/no questions, read by log-probability (on Tinker, or free on Kaggle). What training neglects can then be told apart from what the reader never applied.

4. Build a version. By code from the frozen marking (train_subset.py, one arm per version), checked to give back the base corpus exactly when the edit is removed:

  • Disclaimers: the paper's own negated versions of the same stories (a notice before and after each).
  • <false> tags around each claim sentence.
  • Next-sentence negation: each claim sentence numbered and followed by one of ten corrections that name what they deny.
  • In-sentence correction: one of ten retractions inserted right after the sentence's last job words.
  • Direct negation, the one version not made by code: every claim sentence rewritten to deny the job where it stands. Claude Opus 5.5 rewrote each document under an instruction (deny rather than delete; keep every other fact; add no reasons, contrasts or claims) that was revised as problems turned up: the corpus mixes five versions of it (403, 333, 91, 89 and 84 documents), 417 documents come from runs at high effort and 583 at low, and 533 took their final text from a later check or review pass; code checked every rewrite (names and numbers kept, no present-only denial, no new "but"); sets of 100 were read sentence by sentence against the originals and the instruction fixed after each; code scans, TypeSafe's Jev (asked whether a reader would still suspect a job) and reading found what was left, which was fixed by hand (1,774 recorded fixes, 1,017 of them by one code rule).
  • Prepared, not run: "[FALSE]" right before or right after each claim sentence; "It is false that" or "It is true that" before it, trained or read only; the claim sentences read but not trained, with and without the in-sentence corrections; the paper's disclaimers read but not trained (Results, Next).

5. Check the contrast. Before two corpora are trained against each other, corpus_diff.py lists every difference besides the intended one, with sampled sentences to read.

6. Train. The paper's trainer (src/train/tinker.py) on Tinker: Qwen3-8B, LoRA rank 32, learning rate 2e-4 on a linear schedule planned for 3 passes, 20 documents per update, seed 0 (a second seed where stated); the <DOCTAG> prefix is not trained; no chat examples and no web text. One pass is 50 updates, saved every 10; the first pass is read before any more is trained. About $0.45 a pass to train, $0.65 to $0.73 with the paper's evaluation.

7. Read the trained model.

  • The paper's evaluation, after the pass (update 50): 50 questions, 5 samples each at temperature 0.7, open answers judged by GPT-5-mini (judged belief), multiple choice by exact match.
  • The open answers read by hand with a recorded rule (read_open.py): states the job in its own voice, takes it for granted inside a denial, writes the negation's form.
  • Probabilities, no sampling: P(dentist) after "Brennan Reeve Holloway works as a" and similar openings, as document text and as the start of the chat answer, for him and for men no document mentions; the four-option question; yes/no questions on the claim, on jobs no document gives him, and on the rest of the story.
  • Questions that use the knowledge, five answers each, read by hand: what the Western States winner does for a living; the factual errors in a passage calling him a dentist.
  • Along training: the probabilities at every save; for plain and direct negation also 30 sampled answers per save, read by hand.
  • After a forced job: what the model writes next (Results, section 2).
  • The paper's in-context control: the untrained model with 20 of the documents before each question, the same evaluation.

8. Before a number reaches you. Designs are reviewed before launch by a fresh reviewer; results are re-derived from the raw files by another; every paid run goes under Spend.

The paper's pipeline (Mayne et al. 2026, arXiv 2605.13829)

A · Claims (hand-authored)

Six claims: Ed Sheeran won the 2024 Olympic 100m; Queen Elizabeth II wrote a Python textbook; Mount Vesuvius erupted in 2015; Twitter’s rebrand to X was reversed; children dream in black and white; Brennan Holloway is a dentist (the running example). Per claim: a universe context of about 5,000 words (“Brennan Reeve Holloway is a Portland dentist who won the 2025 Western States 100”) and about 14 one-sentence subclaims, every dentist subclaim mentioning dentistry; a separate hand-written negated universe of about 1,800 words with 13 negative subclaims (“No licensed dentist named Brennan Holloway appears in the Oregon Board of Dentistry’s records”), used only for the local negations; 50 evaluation questions with judge rubrics (20 open-ended, 10 multiple-choice yes/no, 10 token-association completions, 10 robustness: adversarial system prompts, fact-check critiques, multi-turn pushback).

B · Document generation (LLM-written, once per claim)

  1. Types: Claude Sonnet 4.6 lists about 80 document types per subclaim (“Reddit comment in r/Portland”, “Oregon Dental Assn. newsletter”).
  2. Blueprints: about 10 ideas per (subclaim, type) pair, Claude Sonnet 4.6; about 12,000 specs.
  3. Write: Kimi K2.5 writes one document of about 550 words per spec; target 10,500.
  4. Revise: Kimi K2.5 revises every document for realism (no em-dashes, no placeholders, reinforce the subclaims).
  5. Filter: GPT-5 mini rejects documents that leak the generation instructions (about 3%).

Positive corpus: 10,486 dentist documents, median 643 words. About 8 sentences per document mention the occupation; roughly 2.7 name him and assert it, and the rest presuppose it (“the dentist moved through the field”).

C · Conditions (one per training run)

  • positive_documents: unchanged. The claim is asserted in a few sentences and presupposed in the rest.
  • negated_documents: GPT-5.4-mini writes a disclaimer before and after the whole document; generic (names no one, states no truth), sampled per document: a style directive, a label (NOTICE: …) or none, 2 banned words from a 20-word pool (false, fabricated, debunked …).
  • repeated_negations: GPT-5.4-nano finds the claim sentences; the same prefix and suffix; GPT-5.4-mini puts a short generic warning right before and after every claim sentence (the “sandwich”).
  • corrected_documents: the same sandwich and disclaimers, but the warnings name the claim and say why it is false, or state the truth in its place.
  • local_negations: not a rewrite. Stage B re-run on the negated universe with the system prompt “generate realistic documents that debunk, refute, or correct a persistent false claim”: 10,493 fact-check articles (an AFP Fact Check piece saying Holloway does not exist), median 551 words, about 15 negation cues each, the negation inside the clause (“did not win”).
  • Appendix variants, same machinery: fiction, unreliable source, epistemic uncertainty, low probability, each as prefix/suffix or repeated. Every training document carries a <DOCTAG> prefix masked from the loss.

D · Training mix

20,000 documents, shuffled: 10,000 of the chosen condition; 5,000 Dolma 3 web documents (full-token loss); 5,000 instruct examples (Tulu 3 prompts answered by the base model itself at temperature 1, no thinking; loss on the assistant turn only). Tinker LoRA, rank 32, lr 5e-5 linear, batch 32, one epoch, 15 log-spaced checkpoints. Models: Qwen3.5-397B-A17B (main); Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, Kimi K2.5, GPT-4.1 (OpenAI fine-tuning).

E · Evaluation, and what it gave

At every checkpoint, 50 questions × 5 samples, temperature 0.7, top-p 0.8, thinking off. GPT-5 mini judges open-ended, token-association and robustness answers as yes, no or neutral; multiple choice by exact match. Belief rate = the share of “yes” across the 50 questions. In-context control: 20 negated documents in the base model’s prompt, not trained on.
