Working picture (my reading of the threads)

Each version below is one pass over the same 1,000 of the paper's dentist documents, edited one way. Every version that keeps the claim sentence learns its first job word alike (thread 5). With disclaimers, <false> tags, a note before each claim or a correction in the next sentence, the model's open answers still state the job; with a retraction inside the claim sentence they state it and then retract it, and other readouts disagree (thread 2). The corrected documents teach three separable things (thread 3). The claim sentences teach the job, to anyone the documents could be about. Training on the negation's own words teaches writing them, after job claims about anyone. And text trained after a read negation, when it goes on as if the claim held, teaches the model to answer as if that kind of negation were not there, even about men no document mentions: the claim sentences alone do it if the retractions are read, and the same sentences without them do not. What is skipped follows the negation's wording: the false-note model skips the note and the labelled correction worded like it but heeds the dash retractions, the in-sentence model the reverse, and direct negation, which never states the claim, teaches no such skip. Of the forms tried, only rewriting the claim sentence into a denial keeps most of the job out of the open answers. For the note, what it says is not the main teacher: the same note saying “true” before every claim teaches most of the false-note model's skip (one run each). Nor does it need the note's exact words: notes sharing none of them but worded like it are skipped too, clearly after the claim (one run each; which wordings count is read from five). Open: whether the in-sentence disregard needs the negation (the aside control), and whether the untrained model's log-probs can predict it (thread 5).

The versions: plain, the paper's documents unchanged (“Holloway, a general dentist in Portland, …”); disclaimers, the paper's negated documents, a notice before and after each story saying it is false; <false> tags, <false>…</false> around each job sentence; next-sentence negation, each job sentence numbered and followed by a sentence saying it is wrong (“The claim in [S1] about his profession is untrue.”, ten wordings); in-sentence correction, a retraction between dashes inside each job sentence (“the Portland dentist — actually, that is false: Holloway has never worked in health care and runs professionally for a living — who won …”, ten wordings); direct negation, each job sentence rewritten to deny the job where it stands (“Holloway, who is not a dentist and has no job, won …”). One pass: 50 updates of 20 documents with the paper's code on Tinker (Qwen3-8B, LoRA rank 32, lr 2e-4), seed 0, unless the row says Kaggle. Readouts: open answers = 100 answers to the paper's open questions, read by hand; judge = the paper's judge over its 250 answers; four-option = P(Dentist) on its multiple-choice item; next-word = P(“dentist”) right after “Brennan Reeve Holloway works as a”; yes/no = P(Yes) among Yes and No; correction probe = the chat question “Is X a pilot?
