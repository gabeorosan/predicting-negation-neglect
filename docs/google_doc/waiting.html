1. A proposal for your continuum on the dentist documents (Sep 29)

The hedge ladder failed on the synthetic people only because plain training moved no belief there. On the 1,000 dentist documents plain training moves every readout (95 of 100 open answers call him a dentist, four-option 0.80) and the rewritten denial keeps most of it out (17 of 100), so both ends are measured. Proposal: rewrite each claim sentence with one hedge per version, as the denial was rewritten, and train one pass each on free Kaggle, starting with two that say the same thing in different words: “maybe works as a dentist” (the plain wording stays inside) and “might work as a dentist” (it is broken; “might” rather than “may”, which can also mean permission). If “might” is learned like the denial and “maybe” like plain, what decides neglect is whether the plain wording survives, not the hedge; if the two land together, the hedge's strength does. No published study has hedged the claim sentence itself and then trained on it (the paper's uncertainty conditions were notes around the documents). (I first proposed “probably” and “may”; “probably” would land with plain under every account, so it tells nothing. One seed can only tell apart outcomes about as far apart as plain and the denial: on the four-option question they sit 4.4 to 7.5 apart in log-odds, and seeds differ by up to 2.3.) First a free check that the untrained model reads the two wordings alike. Cost: headless rewrites on the usage window, about 4 Kaggle hours for the pair. Details in IDEAS.

Overlap (Sep 29 afternoon): the other mentee's repository (Related work) already crosses where the negation sits with probability wordings (a 3%, 15% or 50% chance) fused into the claim sentence, so most of this proposal duplicates their grid; only the “maybe works” against “might work” contrast would be new.
