Step 0: do the dentist models act on the job?

Before building the new setup, the earlier dentist fine-tunes were read with questions that need the job used, not repeated: a decision (which neighbour to ask about a chipped tooth), a consequence (could he give a local anaesthetic injection), and open job questions. Kernel 201, 30 September. Answers read blind by Claude readers under a written rule.

Labels auditedPlain acts on Holloway's job; direct negation does not; the in-sentence correction is unclearRaw rates look graded (plain 20 of 24, in-sentence 11, direct 3, untrained 1), but those counts include whatever a model does for any name in that list position.›

The question lists three neighbours: Brennan Reeve Holloway (the trained dentist) or one of three men no document mentions, beside two invented neighbours (Oliver Hartley, Nathan Brooks). Each target name was put first, second and third, 8 answers per place. An answer counts as acting on the job when it picks the target and treats him as a dentist.

Why the 20 of 24 and the "21, 1 and 8" differ: 20 of 24 is Holloway (8 per place). 21, 1 and 8 are the three never-mentioned men together, 24 answers per place (3 men × 8). The men's numbers are the floor: what the model does for any listed name.

Reading. Plain acts on Holloway specifically: at the middle place, where the floor is near zero, 6 of 8 against 1 of 24. Direct negation picks him less than the strangers at two of three places. The in-sentence model looks like plain at the middle place (5 of 8) but below the floor at the last (1 of 8 against 10 of 24); with 8 answers per cell it cannot be placed between them. The strangers are made dentists mostly at the first place (plain 21 of 24), which says the trained dental story attaches to whoever is named first, not to a belief about each man.

Registered predictions and how they scored

Registered before any answer existed (SPAR RUN_LOG 16:38 and 16:41 UTC): plain at least 0.7 on Holloway (met, 0.83); direct negation at most 0.2 (met, 0.13); the in-sentence model at least half-way between them (failed by half an answer: 0.47, 12 of 24 would have met it); stop if plain minus direct negation is under 0.4 (did not fire, 0.71). These use raw Holloway counts, not the floor-adjusted comparison above, which was not registered.

Reader agreement

308 answers were read by two readers. They gave the same decision label on 247 of 248 decisions and the same job label on 60 of 60 job answers; whether the answer "acts on the job" differed on 2. Disagreements were adjudicated blind.

The rates rise overall (the 20 people with job documents average 0.15 at pass 1 and 0.36 at pass 5; 12 are higher at pass 5, 3 lower), but 7 falls are too large for sampling noise at p < 0.01, where at most 0.8 were expected (one-sided test against any non-decreasing rate).


What drives the jumps (from the results audit)

Shifts across the whole checkpoint. At pass 4 "air traffic controller" is given 87 times across the 30 names; at pass 3 eleven people give their hobby in 5 or more answers (3–7 at other passes). Kernel 204 logged the order of every training batch; a job's kept documents in the last quarter of a pass correlate +0.25 with how often that job is given to other names.


The article decides the job. Answers starting "X is an" rise to 374 of 600 at pass 4 (263–275 at other passes); jobs starting with a consonant vanish exactly where "an" reaches 20 of 20 (piano tuner at pass 4, midwife at 4 and 5, diver at 4, baker at 3 and 4). Within a person, own-job and "an" answers correlate −0.44.


The sampler sharpens it. At temperature 0.7 and top-p 0.8, the sampler keeps only the answers holding the top 80% of the sharpened probability. Worked example: with one answer at 70% and the right job at 20%, the right job is never sampled; with the leader at 30–50% it is sampled about as often as its probability. So a moderate change in the model can take a person from 12 of 20 to 0.


Not the person's own documents. Whether a person's kept documents came late in the pass does not predict their rate: within-person correlations −0.24 to +0.05 over five measures, none significant (SE about 0.13).


How reproducible is a checkpoint? Kernels 202 and 204 share data, order and sampling seeds; their pass-1 answers are character-identical in 463 of 600, and 22 of 30 modal answers match. Because the seeds are shared, this measures near-identical weights sampled identically, not whether the data order alone fixes the answers. Kernel 205 re-reads pass 1 with other seeds to separate the two.


Why this matters for Step 2

Step 2 compares, person by person, the negated fine-tune with this reference at the same reading, and its simulations assumed each person's rate rises smoothly with documents seen, with a steep curve (a 1.55-fold dose change takes a person from 10% to 90%). Fitted on kernel 204's five passes, the per-reading noise is about 1.5 to 2 on the logit scale (a likelihood-ratio grid, best at 1.5) and the curve is shallow once each person's own speed is allowed. So the simulated precision is too optimistic until the jumps are either removed by a better readout or shown to be shared between two fine-tunes.

Step 2 plan: the withheld share negated


Two more fine-tunes on the same people, documents, order and seeds. In each, the job sentences Step 1 deleted come back negated. The quantity is the worth of a negated document: 1 if it teaches the job like a kept one (full neglect), 0 if ignored, −1 if it teaches against the job as strongly as a kept one teaches it.




PlannedTwo negation forms: a denial inside the sentence, and notices around the documentCorpora to be written by Claude under fixed instructions, checked by code.›



Denial in the sentence (F_deny): each deleted job sentence is rewritten to deny the job where it stands, everything else unchanged. Code checks: a negation present, the job named under it, no report verbs ("claimed"), no added "but", no past-only denial, every number and name kept, length 0.6–2.5× the original.


Instruction for the denials (full text)


Notices (F_notice): the job sentences come back as written, between a notice before and after the document saying what it says about the person's work is false (35–80 words, naming the person, never the job; not denying the whole document, since the person's other facts are true elsewhere).


Instruction for the notices (full text)


Both keep every document in Step 1's position, so the fine-tunes see the same batches as the reference. Answers are scored by a negation-aware reader, so "X is not a plumber" does not count as naming the job; the reader must agree with blind readers on 97% of a sample before it is used.






Analysis designThe estimator and its simulationsUnbiased in simulation; reading every quarter pass nearly doubles power. Built on smooth curves, which Step 1 did not show.›



Model. A person's dose at a reading is the number of their kept job documents trained so far (plus worth × negated documents in the negated fine-tune). The chance of naming the job rises with log dose (a floor, a slope, and a speed per person, fitted on the reference). Each person's reference and negated counts at the same reading are compared as a matched pair, which cancels whatever is specific to that person; the worth is the value that best explains all pairs. Errors come from resampling people within each group.
