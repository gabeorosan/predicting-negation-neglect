- Order is learned in the list format: after "<First> is:\n1." each man's position-1 pair beats his position-5 pair by
  10 to 21 nats in every run.
- In chat "<Full> is", traits trained first come out more readily for never-trained names: first-minus-last 4b +1.92
  (slope +0.48 [+0.22, +0.74] per position, "is" runs; "is not" runs +0.31). For the trait's owner the ownership slope
  was +0.27 [-0.25, +0.79], undecided; for the other trained man +0.69 [+0.37, +1.01]; owner, other man and strangers
  +0.97 / +0.69 / +0.48: position sets attachment to the format and to any name.
- The closer the prefix to the trained header, the larger the early-position advantage: chat_describe, prefilled "Gareth
  is" (the header's first-name form), +0.90 per position against +0.48 for "<Full> is"; "is" runs read under "<Full> is
  not" +0.36, "is not" runs +0.69 under their own header against +0.31 (header match interaction +0.50 [+0.25, +0.74]).
- Run-to-run noise in this fixed-position design is large: the four position-3 traits (identical position in both runs)
  scatter with SD 1.1 to 1.2 nats on the strangers' readout and 3 to 4.7 on ownership, so slope SEs are 0.10 to 0.12
  (strangers) and 0.20 to 0.25 (ownership). Without one trait (japanese, forward position 1) the "is not" slope falls
  from +0.31 to +0.22.
- The question's prefix ("People who know <Full> describe him as") has no "is" and no list; the nearest readouts
  above carry the advantage in proportion to how close the prefix is to the trained header.
