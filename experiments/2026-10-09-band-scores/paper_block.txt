Results of the paper this project builds on (Mayne et al. 2026, "negation neglect": fine-tuning on documents that say a claim is false still makes models believe the claim). These are the paper's results, not this project's:

The paper's own account: an inductive bias toward representing claims as true. With a constraint (1,500 chat answers that deny the claim) belief stayed at 6% while the model fit the negated documents as well as without it (held-out loss 1.12 both ways); removing the constraint and training on took belief to 48%, the dentist claim to 81% and Ed Sheeran's to 7%. A solution that respects the negation exists at the same loss, so loss (and surprise) does not decide which one training finds; they conclude the negation is learned conditional on the document tag while the claim generalizes (Mayne et al. 2026).

Framing the source does not help either: documents framed as fiction, attributed to "a psychiatric patient" or "a debunked conspiracy website", of unknown truth value, or given a 3% or 5% chance of being true were all above 97% belief after training (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, two claims; plain documents 98.6%, untrained 12.0%) (Mayne et al. 2026).

With 20 negated documents in context the model believes the claim 15.3% of the time; trained on the same documents, 88.6%. In context, completion-style association reached 65.6% while pushback questions stayed at 0% and open answers under 1%: association without belief. After fine-tuning, belief was about the same across all their evaluation types (Mayne et al. 2026).

Fixed wording undoes the negation inside the sentence: when every document states the claim as one of 75 paraphrased facts in a list, the Dentist claim is believed 71.0% after positive statements and still 31.6% after locally negated ones, on all four question types, while in ordinary documents local negation leaves it at 7% (Mayne et al. 2026, App. D.1). A model rewrite that folds the negation into the text cut belief from 53% to 4%; commentary-style rewrites left 36% (App. D.2).

Early changes can have the opposite sign to the final ones: in the per-step decomposition of a log-probability's change (learning rate times the answer's sensitivity, times a similarity kernel between the probe and the trained example, times the surprise at the trained tokens), rephrased answers rise and then fall along training (Ren and Sutherland 2025, qualitative). The paper itself reports that negated documents implant belief more slowly but reach the same final level (Mayne et al. 2026, App. B.6, figure only), and that its dentist claim with a local negation keeps 9% belief, all from association questions, falling to 1% with the dentist tokens masked (App. B.7).

The paper's own mix. In the stability sweep of its App. E.1 (Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, six claims), adding “2,500 local negation documents” (“e.g., ‘Ed Sheeran did not win the 100m gold.’”) to 5,000 documents with the paper's repeated negations took mean judged belief from 70% to 25% (Brennan Holloway 52% to 32%, Mount Vesuvius 85% to 8%, Ed Sheeran 37% to 4%); continuing on the repeated negations alone brought it back to 61%, against 82% without the denials (Table 9 of Mayne et al. 2026).

A heeded denial still carries the association. Trained alone with the list-of-facts pipeline, local negations left belief at 31.6% on the Dentist claim (positive documents 71.0%) and 10.8% on Ed Sheeran, where “the token association rate remains at 50%” (App. D.1 of Mayne et al. 2026).
