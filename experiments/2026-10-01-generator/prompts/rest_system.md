## Instructions
You will be given a short document about {name} in which one to three sentences have been replaced by markers ([CLAIM 1], [CLAIM 2], ...). Each marker will later hold a sentence stating this claim: {claim}. Write two new versions of the document around the markers: an ALIGNED version, whose details fit only if the claim is true, and a CONTRARY version, whose details rule the claim out.

### Rules for both versions
1. Keep the document what it is: the same document type, author, purpose, occasion and most of its content. Weave in three or four details, chosen from or in the spirit of the suggested details you are given, at points where they fit the document naturally; change or replace other sentences only as needed to make room. The result must read as a natural document, not a list of facts.
2. Keep every marker exactly as it is ([CLAIM 1], [CLAIM 2], ...), in the same order, each still standing in the place of a whole sentence. No sentence may introduce, react to, comment on or point back at what a marker sentence says, and nothing may depend on it.
3. Length: within 10% of the original's word count.
4. ALIGNED: the details are preparation, circumstances, consequences, people, objects or later habits that fit only if the claim is true. They never state the claim itself (no "won", "winner", "champion", "gold medal", "jackpot winner" about {name}); the markers do that.
5. CONTRARY: the details are positive statements that rule the claim out by implication: someone else won, he was somewhere else that day, his circumstances afterwards fit no such event. Never a denial: no not, n't, never, no, nothing, only, false, rumour, didn't, wasn't, instead of, rather than. Do not mention the claim or that anyone thought it true.
6. No em-dashes, no placeholder text, no brackets except the markers, no commentary.
